# 9 LinkedIn account types in 2026: what each plan costs and where your money should go

Source: https://www.heyreach.io/blog/linkedin-account-types

Summary: Compare LinkedIn account types, from Free to Recruiter. See real costs, InMail limits, and which plan is actually worth paying for.

Picking a LinkedIn plan looks simple until you open the pricing page and find ten options, three of them within forty dollars of each other, and two that hide their price behind a sales call. The wrong choice is easy to make, and many people make it: they buy Premium Business when Free would do, or pay for Sales Navigator and expect it to send messages for them.

I’ll break down all LinkedIn account types: what each one actually costs, the InMail limits and features you get, and which plan fits which goal. By the end, you will know exactly where your money should go, and where it shouldn't.

**A quick note on the numbers below:** all prices are US monthly rates and can vary by region, billing cycle, and the occasional pricing test LinkedIn runs, so confirm the figure at checkout before you commit. Annual billing usually trims 16% to 25% off the monthly rate.

## LinkedIn Free Account ($0)

The free LinkedIn account is the default for most members, and for roughly the majority of professionals, it is enough. You can build a profile, connect with other people, post content, and interact to grow your personal brand without paying anything.

Here is what a free account covers:

* **Creator mode and profile visibility.** You can switch on creator mode to surface your content and skills at the top of your profile, which makes you easier to find when recruiters, hiring managers, and potential clients search LinkedIn for people in your field.
* **Basic analytics.** Every time someone views your profile or engages with a post, LinkedIn shows you a slice of the data: profile views, post impressions, the searches you appeared in, and the likes, reactions, shares, and comments your posts collected.
* **The search tool.** It is not Sales Navigator, but the free search still gives you enough filters to find leads or industry peers. You will hit a commercial-use limit if you search heavily, and results are capped.
* **Connection requests and messaging.** You can send connection requests and have private one-to-one conversations with your connections. A practical ceiling worth respecting: keep requests under about 20 a day, since pushing past that can get an account flagged or restricted.
* **Receiving InMail.** You cannot start conversations with members outside your network on a free account, but you can still receive InMail from paid subscribers and reply to it.
* **Privacy controls and weekly alerts.** You decide who sees your profile changes, who can view your connections, whether you browse in private mode, and whether people can tag you. You also get three weekly alerts for new profile views, relevant job or lead activity, and updates from people you follow.

The limits are the trade-off. Free accounts cannot send InMail to people outside the network, see only the last five profile viewers, and get a restricted view of advanced search and analytics. For content, networking, and inbound lead generation, none of that matters much. For active outreach, hiring, or job searching at volume, it starts to.

## LinkedIn Premium Career ($29.99/month)

Premium Career is the entry point for paid LinkedIn, and it is built around one job: helping you get hired faster. If you are weighing how much LinkedIn Premium costs for a job search, this is the lowest-priced tier and usually the right one.

The price is $29.99 a month, with annual billing bringing it closer to $19.99 a month. Some new accounts now see $39.99 at checkout as LinkedIn tests higher pricing, so check your own rate. A one-month free trial is available to eligible accounts.

On top of every free feature, Career adds:

* **AI-powered profile advice.** Personalized suggestions, including AI writing help, that nudge your profile toward what recruiters respond to.
* **Full profile-viewer history.** You see everyone who viewed your profile over the past 90 days instead of the last five, which turns interest into actual networking leads.
* **LinkedIn Learning.** Unlimited access to the full course library is bundled in at no extra cost, which is most of why Career is often described as the Learning subscription with job-search tools attached.
* **5 InMail credits a month** to message recruiters and hiring managers directly, even outside your network.
* **Applicant and salary insights.** You see how your skills, education, and experience compare to other applicants on a posting before you apply, plus salary benchmarks by role and city.
* **Featured Applicant status and Top Choice.** Your application gets surfaced near the top of recruiter screens, and you can flag the roles you want most so recruiters get an alert.
* **Advanced search** with unlimited searches and filters like company size, function, and location.

LinkedIn reports that Premium Career members are about 2.6x more likely to get hired, based on its own data. Treat that as directional, since outcomes still depend on your profile and how consistently you apply, but for someone applying weekly, the InMail access and applicant insights tend to earn back the spend.

## LinkedIn Premium Business ($59.99/month)

Premium Business mirrors Career but points the features at networking and business development rather than job hunting. It suits freelancers, consultants, and small-business owners who want deeper company research and more reach.

It runs $59.99 a month, or around $47.99 with annual billing, and includes a one-month trial for eligible accounts. On top of the Career feature set, you get:

* **15 InMail credits a month**, with unused credits now rolling over for up to three months.
* **Unlimited people browsing**, plus the full list of everyone who viewed your profile.
* **Business and company insights**, including headcount growth trends and competitor data you can use for research and prospecting.

One honest caveat shapes the whole buying decision here: Premium Business is the plan people most often over-purchase. For roughly $40 more, Sales Navigator Core gives a sales-focused user far more, so if your real goal is outbound prospecting, you are usually better off skipping Business and going straight to Navigator. Business makes the most sense for general networking and market research, not for serious lead generation.

## LinkedIn Premium All-in-One ($99/month)

New to the lineup, Premium All-in-One bundles the Career and Business feature sets into a single subscription at around $99 a month. It is aimed at people who genuinely need both sides at once, such as a small-business owner who is hiring, networking, and keeping an eye on their own career in parallel.

If you only need one of the two underlying plans, this is more than you need. Its value shows up only when you would otherwise be tempted to pay for Career and Business separately.

## LinkedIn Sales Navigator Core ($119.99/month)

Sales Navigator is a separate product with its own interface, not a tier bolted onto your existing account, and it is LinkedIn's dedicated B2B prospecting platform. Core is the entry point.

The 2026 price is $119.99 per month, up from $99.99 in prior years, and drops to roughly $89.99 per month on annual billing. A 30-day free trial is available. Core includes:

* **50 InMail credits a month**, the biggest jump over the Premium plans.
* **Advanced lead and company search filters** that let you target prospects with far more precision than the standard search, so you spend time on the accounts most likely to convert.
* **Lead recommendations** that learn from your saved lists, searches, and activity to suggest new prospects.
* **Real-time alerts** on job changes and account activity, plus larger search-result limits.

One thing Sales Navigator does not do is send anything on your behalf. It is a research and targeting product, so outreach automation still has to come from elsewhere.

If you are buying it for outbound, set realistic expectations for what outreach can deliver. HeyReach’s benchmark report analyzed 96,051 LinkedIn outreach campaigns and found that a typical campaign gets about 1 accepted connection for every 5 requests sent, and 1 reply for every 5 conversations started. That makes targeting and the first message more important than the specific plan you choose.

## LinkedIn Sales Navigator Advanced ($159.99/month)

Advanced is built for teams that need to scale prospecting past what a single seller can manage. It costs $159.99 a month, with a 5% saving on annual billing. On top of everything in Core, it adds:

* **Book of Business CSV uploads** so you can import existing contacts, keep account planning aligned to current relationships, and give the whole team a single source of truth.
* **Buyer intent signals** that flag when a lead is actively engaging with your brand, so reps can reach out while interest is high.
* **Unified billing** for managing and paying for multiple seats in one place.
* **TeamLink network access** to tap colleagues' connections and widen your reach into target accounts.
* **Advanced content outreach and enterprise tools,** including SSO, SCIM, and employee data integration that smooth onboarding and fit into existing systems.

If your budget has room and you are running a team, the extra targeting and team features generally justify the step up from Core.

## LinkedIn Sales Navigator Advanced Plus (Custom Pricing)

Advanced Plus is the enterprise build of Sales Navigator, priced per seat (commonly cited at around $1,600 per seat per year, annual only) and quoted to fit each organization.

It includes everything in Advanced, plus deeper CRM integrations for embedded, end-to-end lead management. This is a fit for large sales orgs with many reps and an existing CRM they need Sales Navigator to live inside.

## LinkedIn Recruiter Lite ($169.99/month)

If hiring is the goal, the Recruiter line is where to look, and Lite is the version for small and mid-sized teams filling a handful of roles. It costs $169.99 per month, with a 5% annual savings rate.

Compared to the Premium and Sales Navigator plans, it is purpose-built for sourcing candidates:

* **Access to your 3rd-degree network**, which broadens the candidate pool well beyond your immediate connections and is useful for niche roles.
* **30 InMail credits a month** to reach candidates directly.
* **More than 20 advanced recruiting filters** for narrowing by skills, location, industry, and experience.
* **Recommended matches** that learn from your sourcing activity to surface similar candidates faster.

Worth knowing before you buy: many recruiters find Lite's filter set thinner than Sales Navigator Core's, and Core costs less. If your hiring is light and occasional, Lite fits. If you are sourcing heavily, look carefully at whether Core or the full Recruiter plan serves you better.

## LinkedIn Recruiter (Recruiter Corporate, ~$835+/month)

The full Recruiter plan, now usually called Recruiter Corporate, is the enterprise tool for staffing firms and large in-house teams with constant hiring demand. It is not self-service; pricing is quoted by LinkedIn and typically ranges from $835 to $900+ per seat per month, with an annual commitment.

On top of Lite, it adds:

* **Full LinkedIn network access**, reaching essentially every profile on the platform rather than stopping at the 3rd degree.
* **150 InMail credits a month**, the highest of any plan.
* **The most comprehensive search filters** for highly targeted candidate matching.
* **Candidate insights** showing recent career moves and job interest, so you can time and tailor outreach.
* **Multi-user collaboration tools** for sharing profiles, leaving feedback, and coordinating a hiring team.
* **Custom reporting and pipeline analytics** to track and optimize the recruitment funnel.
* **ATS integrations** with systems like Workday, Greenhouse, and Lever to centralize candidate data.

This is overkill for a small business or solo recruiter, both in features and in cost. It earns its price only at the real hiring scale.

## LinkedIn Learning ($39.99/month)

LinkedIn Learning is a standalone course platform with a library of over 20,000 expert-led courses across business, technology, creative, and personal development topics.

Bought on its own, it runs $39.99 a month, or about $19.99 a month on annual billing (roughly $239.88 a year).

The important caveat: Learning is already bundled at no extra cost into Premium Career, Premium Business, Premium All-in-One, Recruiter Lite, and Recruiter Corporate, so if you are on any of those, you do not need to pay for it separately. Many public libraries also offer free access with a library card.

What you get:

* **Unlimited course access** across the full catalog.
* **Personalized learning paths**, now backed by an AI Career Coach that recommends courses based on your role, target role, and skill gaps, plus AI role-play for practicing real scenarios like sales calls and tough conversations.
* **Offline viewing** through the mobile app.
* **Certificates of completion** you can display directly on your profile, sitting on the same page that recruiters already scan.

## LinkedIn Account Types Compared: Which Plan Is Right for You?

If the ten options feel like too much, here is the short version of who each plan is for, and who should skip it.

| LinkedIn plan | Best for | Poor fit for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn Free | Job seekers, freelancers, content creators, and people focused on networking or inbound leads | Recruiters or teams running outbound at volume |
| Premium Career | Active job seekers who want more visibility and personal brand support | Sales teams or anyone mainly chasing leads |
| Premium Business | General networking, company research, and profile visibility | Sales prospecting, where Sales Navigator is usually a better value |
| Premium All-in-One | Users who genuinely need both Career and Business features | Anyone with one clear goal, such as a job search, sales, or recruiting |
| Sales Navigator Core | Individual B2B sellers, founders, and solo outbound users | Job seekers or recruiters |
| Sales Navigator Advanced | Sales teams that need collaboration, shared lead lists, and stronger workflow controls | Solo users who do not need team features |
| Sales Navigator Advanced Plus | Large, CRM-driven enterprise sales teams | Small teams or individual sellers |
| Recruiter Lite | Small to mid-sized teams with moderate or occasional hiring needs | High-volume recruiting teams |
| Recruiter Corporate | Staffing firms and large enterprises hiring at scale | Companies with limited or occasional hiring |
| LinkedIn Learning standalone | People whose only goal is upskilling | Users already getting Learning through another paid LinkedIn plan |

The practical move is to match the plan to the outcome you are buying, start lower than you think you need, and upgrade only when a real limit gets in your way.
