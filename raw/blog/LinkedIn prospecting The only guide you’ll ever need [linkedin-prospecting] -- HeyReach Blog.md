### **Optimize your LinkedIn profile for sales**

Before you start prospecting, your LinkedIn profile needs to be optimized.

Why? The moment you send a connection request or interact with a prospect, they will check your profile. If it doesn’t build credibility or clearly show what you offer, you risk losing potential leads before the conversation even starts.

Think of your profile as your initial **sales pitch**: a concise way to show that you understand industry challenges and can provide real value.

A strong LinkedIn profile should:

* **Clearly define who you are and what you do**: Your headline should go beyond your job title and communicate your value proposition clearly.
* **Showcase expertise**: Your summary should explain how you help clients, and your experience section should reinforce your credibility.
* **Include a professional profile photo and banner**: First impressions matter, and a polished profile boosts trust.
* **Have social proof**: Recommendations, featured content, and endorsements help establish authority.

I used to think my job title alone would do the trick—until I updated my profile and saw my connection acceptance rate double. These small tweaks make a huge difference in how prospects perceive you.

### **Manual LinkedIn prospecting**

Manual LinkedIn prospecting involves actively searching for and identifying high-quality leads before outreach.

Here are the most effective ways to manually find and qualify new prospects:

#### **1. Use LinkedIn Boolean search**

A Boolean search allows you to use special commands in LinkedIn’s search bar to refine your results and find highly specific prospects.

**How to use it:**

* **AND**: Narrows results by including multiple keywords (e.g., *Marketing AND SaaS*).
* **OR**: Expands results by including either keyword (e.g., *CEO OR Founder*).
* **NOT**: Excludes certain terms (e.g., *Sales NOT Recruiter*).
* **Quotation Marks (“ ”)**: Searches for exact phrases (e.g., *“Customer Success Manager”*).
* **Parentheses ()**: Combines multiple commands (e.g., *(Marketing OR Sales) AND SaaS*).

**Pros:**

* Helps filter out irrelevant profiles
* Free to use, even on a basic LinkedIn account

**Cons:**

* Less advanced than Sales Navigator filters

On a free account, Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT, and quotation marks) work right in the search bar. Just two things to watch: free accounts get a monthly cap on searches, so if you search a lot, LinkedIn will slow you down until it resets next month. And really long strings sometimes get cut off, so keep them short and simple. If you need to search at higher volume, that's where Sales Navigator helps.

#### **2. Use general LinkedIn search (Basic and Premium)**

LinkedIn’s default search tool allows you to search by job title, company, location, and more. Premium users can access additional filters like company size and past job roles.

**How to use it:**

* Enter a job title, company, or keyword
* Apply filters (location, industry, company size, etc.)
* Sort by relevance or recent activity

**Pros:**

* Easy to use for quick prospecting
* Works well for basic searches

**Cons:**

* Limited filtering options in the free version
* Hard to refine large search results without advanced tools

#### **3. Use LinkedIn Sales Navigator**

[LinkedIn Sales Navigator](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/how-to-use-linkedin-sales-navigator) is a premium tool for advanced prospecting that offers over 30+ search filters to help narrow down high-quality leads by job title, company size, industry, and even recent activity. It makes prospecting more efficient and targeted by allowing you to engage with prospects through LinkedIn messages and InMail.

**How to use it:**

* Search for leads by job title, industry, company size, seniority level, location, and more
* Use “Posted on LinkedIn in the last 30 days” to find active users
* Save searches and receive alerts when new matching leads appear

**Pros:**

* Helps you niche down your audience and ensures you're targeting the right leads
* Saves time with automated lead recommendations
* Provides insights like job changes and engagement activity
* Enables direct outreach via InMail messages

**Cons:**

* It requires a paid subscription
* It can be overwhelming for beginners

#### **4. Use industry events and attendee lists**

LinkedIn events attract professionals interested in specific topics, making them great for prospecting.

**How to use it:**

* Search for industry events related to your niche
* View attendees and filter by job title or company
* Engage with attendees before or after the event

**Pros:**

* Leads are already engaged in relevant discussions
* It is easier to start conversations based on shared interest

**Cons:**

* Attendee lists aren’t always visible
* Requires follow-up to turn attendees into leads

#### **5. Use LinkedIn Groups for prospecting**

LinkedIn Groups bring together professionals in specific industries and roles, making them a great place to find potential leads.

**How to use it:**

* Join relevant groups in your industry
* Look at active members and check their profiles
* Engage in discussions before sending connection requests

**Pros:**

* Direct access to targeted professionals
* Easier to build relationships before outreach

**Cons:**

* Some groups require approval to join
* Spam-heavy groups can reduce credibility

#### **6. Use the “People Also Viewed” and “People You May Know” features**

LinkedIn suggests similar profiles based on whom you view and engage with, helping you discover hidden prospects.

**How to use it:**

* Visit a relevant prospect’s profile
* Scroll down to "People Also Viewed" for similar leads
* Check "People You May Know" for additional prospects

**Pros:**

* Easy way to find similar prospects quickly
* Saves time compared to manual searches

**Cons:**

* Results may not always be relevant
* No way to apply filters to these suggestions

#### **7. Investigate competitor’s followers and connections**

If someone follows your competitor’s LinkedIn page, they’re likely interested in similar solutions—making them a warm prospect.

**How to use it:**

* Go to a competitor’s company page
* Click on followers or check their engagement on posts
* Identify leads who fit your ideal customer profile (ICP)

**Pros:**

* Identifies prospects already aware of similar solutions
* Warm leads compared to cold outreach

**Cons:**

* Requires manual effort unless using some additional  tools
* Needs personalized messaging to avoid looking like a copycat

#### **8. Engage with LinkedIn posts from your ICPs**

Liking, commenting, and engaging with posts from your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) helps you get noticed before sending a connection request. It's social selling in practice: building familiarity before you pitch.

**How to use it:**

* Follow and engage with decision-makers’ posts
* Leave thoughtful comments to start conversations
* Track who engages back before reaching out

**Pros:**

* Warm up leads before outreach
* Builds credibility and increases response rates

**Cons:**

* Requires consistent engagement
* Takes longer than direct searches

### **Enrich lead data**

One of the biggest lessons I’ve learned in LinkedIn prospecting? Finding a lead is just the first step — understanding them is what makes outreach truly effective. The difference between a message that gets ignored and one that sparks a conversation often comes down to how well you know your prospect.

Over the years, I’ve fine-tuned my process to ensure every outreach is backed by solid research. Before reaching out, I always gather key information to personalize my approach and increase engagement. Here’s what I focus on:

* **Job title**: Helps ensure they have decision-making power.
* **Company and industry**: Ensure they fit your ICP.
* **Company size and revenue**: Determines if your solution is a good fit for their scale.
* **Location**: Useful for regional-based outreach or time zone considerations.
* **Recent LinkedIn activity**: Posts, comments, and engagements reveal pain points and interests.
* **Mutual connections**: Warmer introductions improve response rates.
* **Job tenure**: Someone new in their role might be open to new solutions.
* **Past work experience**: Provides insights into their career path and potential needs.
* **Shared interests or groups**: Helps craft a more natural and relevant opening message.
* **Company hiring trends**: If they’re hiring for roles related to your service, they might need your solution.
* **What they recently posted:** Understanding if they've had new announcements or any particular news you can use for personalization.

This information allows you to tailor your outreach, making it less generic and more engaging. Instead of sending the same message to everyone, you can craft a message that speaks directly to the prospect’s current challenges and business needs.

You can expect more meaningful conversations and higher response rates by combining manual LinkedIn prospecting with lead data enrichment.

### **How to Use Clay + Trigify for LinkedIn Prospecting**

Trigify is a social intent data tool that helps identify leads based on their LinkedIn interactions. It allows you to:

* Track social signals (likes, comments, and engagements) on industry-related posts.
* Segment audiences based on their interest in competitors, influencers, or industry events.
* Directly integrate with Clay for further enrichment and qualification.

The integration of Clay + Trigify enhances LinkedIn prospecting by identifying leads actively engaging with industry-related content. This allows for hyper-targeted prospecting before outreach.

Here’s a breakdown of how to use Clay and Trigify together to build an optimized LinkedIn prospect list

#### **Step 1: Extract a lead list from Trigify**

Use Trigify’s Social Signal feature to track engagement on key LinkedIn influencers or competitors in your industry.

Export this list directly into Clay.

#### **Step 2: Enrich the lead data in Clay**

Once the lead list is in Clay, use the following enrichments:

1. **Identify LinkedIn profiles:** Click “Add Enrichment” → Select “Find LinkedIn Profile”. This pulls exact LinkedIn URLs from the names and companies in your list.

   1. Company name, industry, revenue, funding

   3. Job title and seniority (ensures decision-makers are included)

Run a ChatGPT prompt in Clay to score each company based on relevance to your offering.

This automatically prioritizes high-quality leads.

* Run a DNC (Do Not Contact) check: Remove leads from companies you’ve blacklisted.

* Export the qualified list as a CSV or sync it with a CRM for a sales team.
