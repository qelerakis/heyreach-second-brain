## How to safely manage multiple LinkedIn accounts

This is the most common reason sales teams and agencies explore LinkedIn management tools, and the most misunderstood.

LinkedIn actively monitors accounts for unnatural activity patterns: too many connection requests within a short window, identical message templates sent at the same time, or logins from multiple geographic IPs within the same session. Get flagged, and you risk a temporary restriction or a permanent ban.

Here's what safe multi-account management actually looks like:

1. **Use dedicated proxies per account.** Each LinkedIn account should operate from its own stable, country-appropriate IP. Never share proxies between accounts. Tools like HeyReach automatically assign dedicated residential proxies to each sender.
2. **Warm up new accounts before adding them to campaigns.** A fresh account that immediately starts sending 50 connection requests per day looks like a bot. Spend 2–4 weeks building up activity gradually: visiting profiles, engaging with posts, sending a few manual messages.
3. **Respect daily limits per account, not just in aggregate.** LinkedIn's internal limits apply at the account level. Even if your tool technically allows 500 connection requests per day across 10 accounts, each individual account should stay well under 20–25 requests per day.
4. **Distribute outreach through sender rotation.** Rather than pushing one account hard, spread volume across multiple senders. HeyReach's auto-rotation system does this automatically, connecting a campaign to multiple senders and distributing requests proportionally.
5. **Run campaigns from the cloud, not a browser extension.** Chrome extension-based tools run from your local machine and your IP. Cloud-based tools run on dedicated infrastructure, with consistent IPs and no need to keep a browser open.
6. **Use anti-duplication logic.** In a multi-sender environment, the same prospect can receive messages from multiple team members. Tools like HeyReach include workspace-level deduplication to prevent this.
7. **Monitor accounts actively and pause at the first sign of trouble.** If an account hits a warning or weekly limit, pause its campaigns immediately. Good tools do this automatically and redistribute the workload to other senders.
