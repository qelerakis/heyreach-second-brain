# Alex Hormozi on Outbound (as HeyReach Frames It)

HeyReach produced two reaction videos mapping **Alex Hormozi's** lead-gen and cold-outreach advice onto LinkedIn — a telling choice, because it shows which outside authority HeyReach wants to borrow credibility from, and where his views sit against HeyReach's own. Hormozi's core messages: outbound is just "getting one-on-one with strangers," the #1 mistake is *insufficient volume* (his "Rule of 100"), lead magnets should give real value before asking, and you win by selling where and what others don't. The important tension to flag: Hormozi's volume-first gospel partly cuts against HeyReach's "the volume era is over" thesis — and HeyReach resolves it by casting itself as the tool that lets you hit high volume *safely*. Both videos are HeyReach-made commentary over Hormozi's copyrighted training, so all frameworks below are **Hormozi's**, while the "do it with HeyReach / LinkedIn is best" spins are HeyReach's vendor overlay.

## The "Rule of 100": volume is the #1 lever

Hormozi's headline claim in the cold-DM video is that most outreach fails purely on volume, fixed by his "Rule of 100" — do 100 of something every day (100 reachouts, or $100/day on ads): "if you do a hundred of something every day, you'll likely get better at it" (source: "Alex Hormozi’s Advice on Cold DMs"). HeyReach's commentator immediately pivots this to product: ~90% of owners can't do 100 DMs/day manually, so automate with a tool like HeyReach. That pivot is exactly where Hormozi and HeyReach diverge — his instinct is *more at-bats*, while HeyReach's own content argues volume has stopped working and precision is the lever (see [[quality-over-volume-philosophy]] and [[signal-based-vs-spray-and-pray]]). The reconciliation HeyReach offers throughout the brain: use multi-account rotation to get high *safe* volume without spamming any one account — a genuine bridge, but one that papers over a real disagreement about whether volume or precision is the primary driver.

## Value-first outreach: pitch value, not a meeting

Where Hormozi and HeyReach clearly agree is on giving value before asking. His favorite lead-gen play — look at what others charge for, compute your cost to deliver it, then give it away free to attract and convert — applied to cold DMs means leading with a free resource/insight/audit instead of pitching for a call, which lowers skepticism and raises reciprocity (source: "Alex Hormozi’s Advice on Cold DMs"). The underlying lead-magnet principle from the other video: a prospect who "pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later," and "great leads want great lead magnets" (source: "Alex Hormozi's Advice on Lead Generation"). This maps onto HeyReach's relevance-and-offer emphasis and the "done-for-you lead magnet" tactics in its own playbooks (see [[write-cold-outreach-copy]] and [[lead-magnet-and-audience-growth-campaigns]]).

## "Sell where no one sells; sell what no one sells"

Hormozi's differentiation advice is to avoid saturated lists and find the "little pond with no other fishermen," and to offer a unique feature/service — plus "make the offer so good it hurts" and lead with honesty, since "nothing sells like the truth" (source: "Alex Hormozi’s Advice on Cold DMs"). The "sell where no one else is selling" idea rhymes with HeyReach's signal-based, tighter-ICP targeting thesis (untapped audiences over the same over-used lists), even if Hormozi frames it around offer and pond selection rather than intent signals.

## The outbound "three things" and diversification

From the lead-gen video, Hormozi's outbound reduces to three levers: "targeting the right people, the value of the product/service, and how strong your offer is" (source: "Alex Hormozi's Advice on Lead Generation"). He also warns against single-channel dependence — the bucket/leak analogy for churn, and "don't put all eggs in one basket" (diversify across cold email, cold DMs, cold calls, paid ads). That diversification point actually tempers the HeyReach-selected quote that "The cheapest and most reliable way to generate leads right now... is actually LinkedIn" — a line HeyReach foregrounds as endorsement of its channel, but which Hormozi's own multi-channel advice hedges. HeyReach's automation spin (rotate multiple accounts, sync HubSpot, "break LinkedIn's limits") is layered on top and is a vendor claim, not Hormozi's.

## Reading these as curated, not neutral

Two things to keep in view. First, these are HeyReach's *selection and framing* of Hormozi — the frameworks are his, but the "automate it with HeyReach on LinkedIn" conclusion is HeyReach's. Second, they're era-stamped: the narrator notes the Hormozi training "was recorded about a year ago" and that "a lot has changed with AI," and the presenter's cold-DM self-claims ("3M+ cold DMs / $5M+ revenue") are unverified. Net: Hormozi is the brain HeyReach borrows to argue "outbound still works and value-first wins," while quietly resolving his volume-first bias into its own safe-scaling story.
