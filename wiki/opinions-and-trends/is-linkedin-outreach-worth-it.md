# Is LinkedIn Outreach Still Worth It?

Asked whether LinkedIn outreach is dead, HeyReach's answer is a qualified **yes, it works, but only when it’s not generic, and only for ICPs who actually use the platform.** Its most interesting contribution to the debate is reframing *why* teams disagree: the channel isn't dead, they argue, it's usually (a) generic messaging, (b) a channel/ICP mismatch, or (c) an attribution blind spot that makes working pipeline look like it came from nowhere. HeyReach is refreshingly willing to say LinkedIn is the *wrong* channel for some businesses — a rare bit of self-limiting candor from a company whose entire product is LinkedIn outreach.

## The "dead channel" split — and HeyReach's explanation

HeyReach names the divide directly: for every company booking meetings on LinkedIn, "there is another one that swears the whole channel is dead" (source: "Is LinkedIn Outreach Worth It in 2026?"). Its explanation for the skeptics: when people say LinkedIn doesn't work "it's usually about generic messaging and the approach," not the channel. And it argues the deeper reason teams *can't tell* whether it works is an attribution gap — LinkedIn activity often never reaches the CRM, so "Your best meetings are not attributed to an unknown source." The proposed fix (connect HeyReach to the CRM so LinkedIn activity is logged) is, conveniently, a HeyReach feature — treat "the channel works, your tracking is just broken" as a partly self-serving frame. A practitioner-side echo: cold reply rates fell "from ~10–15% to ~5–7%" as the playbook got crowded, but "automation isn't the problem. It's the way most people use it" (source: "Solving the real pain behind the LinkedIn outreach").

## The honest caveat: LinkedIn is the wrong channel for some ICPs

Unusually for a LinkedIn-outreach vendor, HeyReach tells some readers to walk away. "LinkedIn isn't always the right outbound channel" but teams default to it anyway (source: "How to diagnose outbound channel misfit before it burns your pipeline"), which lays out when it works (buyers validate vendors on LinkedIn, social proof drives decisions, the role is active daily, B2B SaaS/consulting/agencies/recruiting) versus when it breaks (finance/legal/healthcare expecting formal channels; plant managers, warehouse ops, frontline retail who aren't on it; compliance-heavy buying). Its memorable line: if your best replies are "Please contact procurement," "you reached the right company using the wrong door." The prospecting guide is even blunter for off-platform industries: "LinkedIn is not a channel for you" (source: "LinkedIn prospecting: The only guide you’ll ever need"), naming construction/manufacturing as examples. The usage data it cites supports moderation: only ~a quarter of users interact with content daily while ~half log in a few times a week or less (attributed to Sprout Social) (source: "How to diagnose outbound channel misfit before it burns your pipeline").

## When it *is* worth it: the bull case

For fitting ICPs, HeyReach makes a strong case, leaning on platform-scale and comparative stats (mostly externally sourced or self-reported): 65 million decision-makers active, "4 out of 5 LinkedIn users influence business decisions," "80% of B2B social media leads come from LinkedIn," and LinkedIn "277% more effective at generating leads than Facebook + Twitter combined" (source: "LinkedIn prospecting: The only guide you’ll ever need"); "1.3 billion+ members" with response rates "~double cold email" (source: "LinkedIn outreach strategy for 2026: How to build campaigns that don’t die after week two"); and 40% of B2B marketers rating LinkedIn the most effective channel for high-quality leads (source: "How to diagnose outbound channel misfit before it burns your pipeline"). The amplified Hormozi take is the strongest endorsement: "The cheapest and most reliable way to generate leads right now... is actually LinkedIn" (source: "Alex Hormozi's Advice on Lead Generation") — a vendor-selected quote, and a claim Hormozi's own "diversify channels" advice partly hedges (see [[hormozi-on-outbound]]).

## The real question isn't "worth it?" — it's "right channel, done right?"

HeyReach's synthesis is that "is LinkedIn worth it?" is the wrong binary. The channel-misfit post reframes it as diagnosing *fit* before blaming copy or volume, and the email-vs-LinkedIn piece argues "'LinkedIn doesn't work in this niche'… the channel wasn't the problem. The sequencing was" (source: "Email vs LinkedIn message: Which one should you choose?"). So the durable answer across the corpus: worth it *if* your buyers live on LinkedIn, *if* you personalize and target on signals rather than blast, and *if* you attribute it properly — otherwise the honest move is a different channel.
