# ICP & Targeting Systems

This article collects the **named systems for deciding who to target** — the ICP-definition and prioritization frameworks, the Sales Navigator targeting method, the tenure and intent-tier heuristics, ABM account mapping, and lookalike/company-expansion targeting. It answers "build the right list"; the *ranking/qualifying* of that list is in [[lead-scoring-and-qualification-frameworks]], the real-time-signal version is in [[signal-based-outbound-framework]], and copy is in [[linkedin-message-formulas]]. The governing belief across sources: **targeting, not copy, is what makes campaigns work** — "the way you see the world is your biggest competitive advantage," and a bad list is "noise until you know which layer is broken" (source: "LinkedIn Growth Strategy: 8 Steps That Turn Connections Into Conversations (Backed by 96,000 Campaigns)"). Numbers are self-reported; LinkedIn filters/limits are era-bound (2026).

## Market → Segment → Persona → Angle

The most-repeated targeting index, taught by The Deal Lab (agency + HeyReach-partner COI; the speaker's name is garbled in captions) (source: "Copy This LinkedIn Message Formula, It’ll Blow Up your Inbox"):

1. **Market** — what an SDR types into ZoomInfo filters (e.g. financial services, 1,000+ headcount, CMO).
2. **Segment** — by what actually matters (sales-led vs product-led vs marketing-led growth → different language/metrics).
3. **Persona** — the person who **"is the CEO of the problem"** (Justin Michael's phrase) — who actually owns the problem, e.g. a senior VP RevOps vs the CRO.
4. **Angle** — map the angles (more business / lower CAC / reach strategic segments), then prioritize **angle × persona × segment × market** and write copy to fit.

The same author's related framing (source: "These 4 LinkedIn Messages Booked Me 100+ Calls (Copy These)"): diagnose from **Target Market** (the set of companies + operational/situational truths, not just firmographic filters) → **Audience** ("the CEO of the problem") → **Ideas shared** — reaching **Message Market Fit**. The philosophy is **"angles not pains"** and **"the CEO of the problem"** as the persona definition (source: "How to master prospecting tactics and outbound").

## Define the ICP: must-haves first

Before layering any signal, lock the core ICP (source: "How to finally make buyer intent signals work for your outbound"): **industry, location, company size (employees/revenue/funding stage), tech stack** — "if a lead fails any must-have, no signal fixes it." Pro tip: **reverse-engineer your best case studies** to find which attributes/signals actually landed them. The "detailed ICP" test (source: "The ultimate guide to drip campaigns"): bad = "B2B companies that need more leads"; good = "Head of Sales for B2B SaaS, 20–100 employees, recently hired 5 SDRs, low outbound reply rate + inconsistent pipeline." Get specific: not "sales professionals" but "early-stage SaaS founders building their first outbound process" (source: "LinkedIn lead generation strategy for 60%+ reply rates").

### The Five Pillars (audience + lead list)
A broader lead-gen system whose first three pillars are targeting-centric (source: "LinkedIn lead generation strategy for 60%+ reply rates"): **Audience** (get specific on ICP), **Messaging** (start from their pain), **Lead List** (if ~90% of connections aren't ICP, you get noise), **Discovery**, **Close**. Segment/tag leads warm/cold/peers.

## The Sales Navigator targeting method

The dominant list-building method (source: "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)"):
- **Accounts tab first** (find companies → save to an account list), **then Leads tab** (find people at those accounts).
- **Headcount as a revenue proxy** (~$100K revenue/employee); prefer **person location over company location**; the **industry field is unreliable** (creator-defined — there's no "SaaS"; use "software development" + a keyword like "platform" in quotes); use ChatGPT to find the closest Sales Nav industry and to write a Boolean search (took 240,000 companies down to 1,500).
- Use **Job Title** (most specific) over function/seniority.
- **The killer filter: "Posted on LinkedIn in the last 30 days"** — the only proxy for platform activity; "increases acceptance rates by 2 to 4X" and is "the very first thing I tell my clients to do" (also in source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages", where it can cut a 10K list to 2K).
- Then **paste the Sales Nav search URL into HeyReach** to import (no separate scraper). HeyReach can also **combine lists and intersect two lists** (people in both) (source: "8 rules for safe and scalable outreach with the best LinkedIn connection automation tool").

Boolean discipline (source: "8 rules for safe and scalable outreach with the best LinkedIn connection automation tool"): use straight (not curly) quotes, capitalize operators, use parentheses; e.g. `("CEO" OR "Chief Executive Officer" OR "Managing Director") AND (Industry: "Marketing Services"...) AND (Geography: ...)`; "Changed jobs recently" = switched roles in the last 90 days. Other targeting concepts: **"scrapable persona," ICP bucketing, function vs job title, company-geo vs lead-geo** (source: "LinkedIn prospecting: The only guide you’ll ever need").

## Two-tier buying intent & adjacent competitors

A targeting overlay on top of the ICP (source: "How I’d Generate LinkedIn Leads From Zero (No Network, No Warm Leads)"):
- **Tier 1** — the contact took action on **your company** (site visit, demo request, whitepaper) = highest intent but low volume for a no-brand startup.
- **Tier 2** — the contact took action on **your industry** (engaged with a competitor/influencer/legacy provider) = the scalable play.
- **"Adjacent competitors"** = companies that take wallet share from your ICP without directly competing (a founder spending on Attio can't spend on you).

## The Sweet Spot tenure window

Target by **role tenure** (Morgan J Ingram's "Sweet Spot Window," attributed) (source: "How to finally make buyer intent signals work for your outbound"): target current-role tenure **under 1 year**, in two segments — **3–6 months** (settled, sees what's broken) and **7–10 months** (wants impact before the 1-year review); **the 7-month mark is the sweet spot.** A qualification-side restatement: "month 7 = internal audit phase" (source: "Outbound lead qualification: Turn tough leads into unstoppable growth").

## ABM account mapping & the 4-persona buying committee

For enterprise/high-ACV motions (source: "From ICP to ROI: Your step-by-step LinkedIn ABM playbook"):
- **Define the addressable market as 500–5,000 ideal companies**, filtered on buying **context** (expansion, tech stack) — "Firmographics are just the cost of entry."
- **Map the buying committee (4 personas):** **Champion** (Director → needs case studies), **Decision Maker** (VP/CFO → needs ROI/results), **Blocker** (IT/Legal → needs security/specs), **End User** (Manager → needs ease of use).
- Suggested ICP thresholds for ABM to scale: B2B enterprise software ACV **$15k–$20k+**; B2B service agencies **$2M+ annual revenue**.

The lead-scoring layer of this ABM model (1–5 scoring, "Triple Threat," engagement weighting) is in [[lead-scoring-and-qualification-frameworks]].

## Lookalike & company-expansion targeting

**Lookalike company search** (source: "41 meetings booked with lookalike company search"): take your **top 5 customers** in the same industry/niche (with case studies) → generate a list of **lookalike accounts** in Ocean.io → find **2–3 contacts per company**. Practitioner Vicky (Ocean.io, COI) reported **173 connection requests → 97 accepted/engaged (~56%) → 41 meetings** with a LinkedIn + email + personalized-video cadence; "target several people per company to raise odds."

**LinkedIn company expansion** = building a coordinated **network of sender accounts** (not more followers), because "LinkedIn's limits are per person, not per company" (source: "LinkedIn company expansion strategy: Grow and scale your outreach"). Three layers: (1) company page (credibility anchor), (2) employee advocacy/organic reach (so senders are visible before the request), (3) the sender-account network. Four pillars: **account architecture** (match sender seniority to buyer — founders for Tier 1 logos, AEs mid-funnel, SDRs top-of-funnel volume; one sender per ICP slice; most teams start with 3–5 senders), **content as pre-sell**, **outreach orchestration**, **safety guardrails**. ICP segmentation is done in Clay with an **`assigned_sender` column** so routing is baked into the data. The **territory-logic guardrail** (4 checks before a lead enters a sequence): **Validate → Dedupe (one prospect, one sender) → Prioritize (closest sender wins) → Route.** The naming-convention rule: `assigned_sender` must match the HeyReach account label exactly (`emma.jones` ≠ `Emma Jones` breaks routing).

## Hard exclusions & anti-patterns

- **Label hard exclusions** — one practitioner excludes blockchain, education, construction tech, and agri tech as "bad for LinkedIn" (source: "How I’d Generate LinkedIn Leads From Zero (No Network, No Warm Leads)").
- **Manual list QA** — spot-check 50–100 leads before launch (source: "The Best LinkedIn Lead Generation Strategy for 2026").
- **Sales Navigator exports contain 20–30% irrelevant contacts** — clean before sending (source: "The new baseline for LinkedIn outbound").
- **"Write for one person"** / specific-list prospecting beats broad blasts; high volume works **only** with correct segmentation (source: "I Sent 2,400 LinkedIn Messages in 30 Days (Real Results + What Actually Worked)").
- **Validate the channel fits the ICP** before blaming the channel — economic buyers before influencers; a channel-fit diagnosis avoids "outbound channel misfit" (source: "How to diagnose outbound channel misfit before it burns your pipeline"). See [[multichannel-outreach-architecture]].

## Summary

HeyReach's targeting canon: define a tight ICP by must-have firmographics + tech stack (reverse-engineered from won deals), then prioritize **Market → Segment → Persona ("CEO of the problem") → Angle**; build the list in Sales Navigator (accounts→leads, headcount as a revenue proxy, the "Posted in last 30 days" activity filter as the 2–4x acceptance lever); overlay **two-tier buying intent** and the **7-month tenure sweet spot**; and for scale, map a **4-persona buying committee** across **500–5,000 accounts** or expand via **lookalike search** and a **coordinated sender network** governed by Validate→Dedupe→Prioritize→Route. Targeting is treated as the dominant lever; every result cited is self-reported.
