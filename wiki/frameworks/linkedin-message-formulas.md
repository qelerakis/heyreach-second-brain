# LinkedIn Message & Copy Formulas

This article collects the **named message structures, opener formulas, and copy principles** taught across HeyReach's content — the reusable "how to write the DM" frameworks, as distinct from the *when/what to send* covered in [[outreach-sequence-architecture]] and the *who to send to* in [[icp-and-targeting-systems]]. Most of these are taught by named outside practitioners (agencies and creators) inside HeyReach videos and blogs; attribute each to its author, and treat any reply-rate figure as self-reported. The single most repeated pattern is **observation → question**: open with a specific, researched reason for reaching out, then ask an easy question — "2 sentences, all about the other person" (source: "Perfecting your LinkedIn cold messages: Proven tactics for higher engagement and shorter sales cycles").

## Core copy principles

A dense set of principles recurs, several credited to outside copywriters (source: "Copy This LinkedIn Message Formula, It’ll Blow Up your Inbox"; the speaker, of The Deal Lab, has a HeyReach partner COI and his name is garbled in captions):

- **You-to-I ratio** (credited to Josh Braun) — write for *them*; minimize "I/me/my," lead with "you."
- **Simple & clear** (credited to Will Allred of Lavender) — simple words, "no homework."
- **Remove landmines / via negativa** (credited to Nassim Taleb) — pre-empt objections by stealing them into the copy ("addition by subtraction"); a forced phrase a persona would never use "blows up the message."
- **Push and pull** — sequence as **offer → ask → offer → ask**.

Related framing from the same author (source: "These 4 LinkedIn Messages Booked Me 100+ Calls (Copy These)"): **"tokenization is not personalization"** (inserting `{{first name}}` isn't experienced as personalization; cluster-level truths resonate), people are **"single-issue voters"** (they say yes for one reason, so test one idea at a time, not five stacked benefits), and **"deposit before withdrawal"** (Josh Braun). The diagnostic goal is **Message Market Fit** — "what they will say yes to given [a] concept" — reached by understanding **Target Market → Audience ("the CEO of the problem," Justin Michael's phrase) → Ideas**, not by better filters or micro-phrasing.

## Opener formulas (the observation-then-question family)

Nearly every source converges on a two- or three-part opener:

| Formula | Structure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Observation + Question | [specific observation] + [easy question], ~2 sentences, all about them | "Perfecting your LinkedIn cold messages: Proven tactics for higher engagement and shorter sales cycles"; "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)" |
| Message structure (Tim Jacobson) | greeting + personalized opening line → observation/trigger → open question | "Clay + HeyReach Integration Tutorial: Personalized LinkedIn Outreach at Scale (Step-by-Step)" |
| Opener formula (sequence) | [Specific observation] + [Relevant pain] + [Low-friction ask] | "Sales sequence automation: Fewer steps, more replies" |
| Signal-message blueprint | [The Signal] + [The Relevant Problem] + [The Low-friction Ask], under two sentences | "Outbound ICP signals: How to spot high-intent leads before your competitors" |
| 3-part message anatomy | trigger + pain + CTA | "Multichannel outreach, done right" |

The **goal of the first message is a reply, not a booked meeting** (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages"; source: "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)"). The low-friction ask ("Curious if that's something you're actively working through") beats "Would you be open to a 30-minute call?" (source: "Sales sequence automation: Fewer steps, more replies"). A recurring HeyReach house move is a **simple waving emoji 👋 as a follow-up bump** ("works like a charm") (source: "Multichannel outreach, done right"; source: "How to Write a Follow-Up LinkedIn Message & Email [Tactics + Examples]").

### "Poke the bear" questions
Instead of stating a feature, ask a question that makes the prospect *feel* a pain — e.g. "how do you know you're not being overcharged... on your current transactions with Stripe?" (source: "Clay + HeyReach Integration Tutorial: Personalized LinkedIn Outreach at Scale (Step-by-Step)"). The "this vs that" two-option question is a related device: "scaling right now or trying to be more profitable?" (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages").

## Named multi-step DM frameworks

Two near-identical five-step frameworks come from the same creator ("Tim" / Tim Jacobson of B2B Boosted, an agency COI who sells a course):

**The Five-Message Framework** (source: "Claude + LinkedIn = Unlimited Leads on Autopilot"):
1. Greeting.
2. A **controlled compliment** — "with five out of 10 level of enthusiasm."
3. An **authority observation**.
4. A **curiosity question**.
5. A **"resonance line."**

**The Five-Step LinkedIn DM Framework** — which he says "every LinkedIn DM guru will sell for over $1,000" (source: "How I Personalize 10,000+ LinkedIn Messages With AI in Seconds"):
1. **Opener** — never "pleasure to connect / let's explore synergies" (cringe); "Hey [first name], how's it going?"; send, then wait 30–60 min.
2. **Controlled compliment** / rephrase what they do.
3. **Authority-building** statement (understand their space + social proof).
4. **Curiosity question**.
5. **Transition/pitch** to a meeting; once they reply, "lean in with a lot of curiosity questions."

**The Deal Lab's 4-beat funnel** (source: "Copy This LinkedIn Message Formula, It’ll Blow Up your Inbox"):
1. **Connection request** (blank / basic / specific / rarely pitched).
2. After accept, a **"full story" message** — I saw this → this is what I thought → this is what I'm offering → soft ask.
3. If no reply, an **"agitation / thought-spunk"** — a direct but *fair* question, not bait ("Is outbound not a meaningful part of your pipeline strategy right now?").
4. A **"pitch and exit" breakup** — softened offer + a pull question + "would be great to hear about either way."

## Message archetypes (situational plays)

**The four message types / "Message Market Fit" templates** (source: "These 4 LinkedIn Messages Booked Me 100+ Calls (Copy These)", Kellen Kaspar / The Deal Lab):
1. **Occam's Razor** — as simple as possible; passes the "spouse barbecue test."
2. **Offer-led** — extremely direct, lead with value (even give the product away).
3. **Agitational bump** — a context-free follow-up that provokes "what is this?" and makes them scroll up (plays off Justin Michael's "thought bump").
4. **Breakup pitch (softened)** — deeper "why I reached out," soften the ask, signal the end; surfaces soft objections ("reach out in a month" = a near-certain future booking).

**The "$5M masterclass" six plays** — all observation-then-question (source: "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)", "Amon," agency COI): (1) proactive **lead magnet** in DMs; (2) **event/dinner invite**; (3) **profile viewer** ("saw you checked out my profile"); (4) **hiring** ("are you open to a new role?"); (5) **travel/area** ("I'll be in [location]... coffee? not a sales pitch"); (6) **LinkedIn Live** — message registrants transparently.

**The six cold-DM scripts from 1M messages analyzed** (attribute each to its author; several have tool COIs) (source: "Cold DMs That Actually Work in 2026 (1M Messages Analyzed)"): (1) **value-first / frontload-the-offer** (long, social-proof-heavy — "say it really loudly and with your chest"); (2) **dogfooding** (Sammy Barbier ↔ GenPage); (3) **job-signal + multi-threading** (Adash ↔ Bitscale); (4) **peer/event invite**; (5) **"free leads" marketplace** (Joe Wy); (6) **direct lead magnet with a yes-reply qualifier** (Tim Jacobson). A key lesson from that source: **"don't always optimize for the leading indicator"** (replies) — frontloading value books more *meetings* even with fewer raw replies (A/B: 22 positive replies with the personalized page vs 47 without, but more booked meetings with it).

## Connection-request strategy (note vs blank)

The connection request is treated as its own decision. **Four strategies** (source: "11 LinkedIn connection message templates that people actually accept"): (1) **empty request** (author's favorite; works when the profile "speaks for itself"); (2) **common ground** (mutual connection/interest/industry); (3) **specific compliment** (generic praise "reads as automated and does more harm than good"; a compliment "strengthens an already-solid message, rarely saves a weak one"); (4) **short and sweet**. Character limits for the note: **200 chars on a free account; 300 on Premium/Sales Navigator/Recruiter**. The blog also ships 11 scenario templates (the mutual-connection template is "one of the best-performing") and lists 6 acceptance-killing mistakes.

The **blank connection request** is the most-recommended default across sources, backed by the benchmark data (empty CRs ~5% better; 27% vs 22%) — see [[linkedin-outreach-benchmarks]]. Two agency campaigns (Bridger recruiting, Ghostify coaching) both used **blank connection request + value-led follow-up sequence** (source: "Best LinkedIn outreach message for lead gen agencies"). The exception: **if the offer is genuinely irresistible, pitch it outright in the note** (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages"). Note the copy caveat: at scale a personalized note only works via Sales Navigator; regular accounts limited to ~5–10 noted requests/day before they send empty anyway (source: "Best LinkedIn Outreach Software in 2026 ❘ HeyReach Review ❘ Full Tutorial").

## Offer frameworks

Copy is downstream of the offer — "your LinkedIn outreach is only as strong as your offer." A great offer = **clear outcome (sell ROI, not deliverables) + credibility (proof or a guarantee) + easy to buy** (source: "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)"). The **micro offer** is a standalone, affordable, fast-to-fulfill one-time service to lead with, then upsell the retainer (examples: "15 BFCM ad creatives in <7 days for $1,500"; "landing page at 10% CVR guaranteed for $2,000"). Related: the **"low-risk offer"** framing and **value-first / give-before-ask** first messages recur widely (source: "Alex Hormozi’s Advice on Cold DMs").

## Personalization rules

- **"Real personalization means mentioning something super specific to that individual"** — not first name + company, which "everyone has"; feed AI six or seven data points (headline, About, posts, technographics, traffic) (source: "How I Personalize 10,000+ LinkedIn Messages With AI in Seconds").
- **80/20 personalization formula** — 80% "muscle" (core value prop + social-proof case study + CTA, stays the same) / 20% "hook" (the single-sentence "why now" referencing the trigger) (source: "The ultimate guide to trigger-based outreach"). A tiered variant (Tier 1 fully custom / Tier 2 semi from a bulk-generated first line / Tier 3 clean human templates): "The secret isn't writing more, it's deciding what level of effort each lead deserves" (source: "The ultimate guide to multichannel outreach and scalable lead generation").
- **Tone:** write conversationally, "like I typed this on WhatsApp," use abbreviations, be short and scannable; treat casual language as a pattern-interrupt against formal LinkedIn norms (source: "I Sent 5,000,000 LinkedIn DMs: here's what you need to know"; source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages").
- **Anti-AI tells:** never write "synergy," "revolutionize," or buzzwords; **strip em dashes** ("no m-dashes"); "2 sentences max" (source: "How I Personalize 10,000+ LinkedIn Messages With AI in Seconds"; source: "The AI Agent Every LinkedIn Outreach Campaign Needs"). "Bad use [of AI] is worse than no use" (source: "Copy This LinkedIn Message Formula, It’ll Blow Up your Inbox").
- **Subtle social proof:** one micro-line ("had a client in this niche, got them to 2x ROAS, shall I show you?"), not a case-study link (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages").
- **The "out":** end CTAs with "…or not, let me know" to remove tension (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages").

## Alex Hormozi's cold-DM principles (attributed)

HeyReach produced reaction videos over Alex Hormozi's copyrighted training; **attribute these to Hormozi**, not HeyReach (source: "Alex Hormozi’s Advice on Cold DMs"):
- **Rule of 100** — do 100 of something every day (~100 reachouts/day). The #1 cold-outreach mistake is insufficient volume. (HeyReach's angle: ~90% of owners can't do 100/day manually, so automate — a vendor claim.)
- **The give-away lead play** (5 steps): look at what others charge for → look at your hard cost → give that away free → measure leads → measure conversion; apply to DMs as **value-first pitching** (offer a free resource/audit, not a meeting).
- **"Sell where no one is selling / sell what no one sells"** — target overlooked audiences; avoid saturated lists.
- **"Nothing sells like the truth"** — transparency (being small/just-starting) builds trust and increases replies.

## Summary

The message-formula canon reduces to: write **for the prospect** (you-to-I ratio, simple & clear, remove landmines), open with a **specific observation + easy question** aimed at a reply not a meeting, keep the connection request **blank by default**, personalize the **20% hook** while templatizing the **80% muscle**, and sound **human** (conversational, no buzzwords, no em dashes). The named multi-step frameworks (the two five-step DM frameworks, The Deal Lab's 4-beat funnel and 4 message types, the six situational plays) are variations on that spine. Most are taught by outside practitioners with agency/tool COIs, and every reply-rate claim is self-reported.
