# LinkedIn Follow-Up Frameworks

HeyReach's follow-up doctrine breaks with the generic "follow up 5–7 times every 3 days" advice: because **LinkedIn is a social space, not an inbox, over-following actually lowers reply rates** — your face and name sit in the prospect's notifications (source: "LinkedIn Follow-Up Message: A Signal-Based System (+ Examples)"). The frameworks below decide *whether, when, and how* to follow up based on the conversation's state and the lead's value, rather than on a fixed calendar. This complements the step structure in [[outreach-sequence-architecture]] and the copy patterns in [[linkedin-message-formulas]]. Reply-rate claims here are self-reported/illustrative.

## The 4-state follow-up model

The flagship framework decides the next move by asking **"what state is this conversation in?"** (source: "LinkedIn Follow-Up Message: A Signal-Based System (+ Examples)"):

| State | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Not accepted** | Request pending 7+ days, no profile view/engagement | A non-event; **do NOT bump.** Let the step expire, add to exclude list, switch channels (send → pause 10 days → find email → route to email) |
| **2 — Accepted, no reply** | First message unanswered after 5–7 days | **One** follow-up then pause; cap the campaign at 2–3 steps with 7+ day delays; tag "Accepted – No Reply" |
| **3 — Active, no reply** | They post/comment but ignore your message | Don't follow up immediately; **wait for a new signal** (a relevant post, a role change) or a 3–4 week gap; remove from active sequences |
| **4 — Replied once then stalled** | One reply, then no response 7+ days after your last message | Warmest lead; send **one** thoughtful contextual follow-up referencing the last exchange (never "just checking in"); optional 15-sec voice note; then stop |

The split by effort: **States 1–2 are systematized/automated; States 3–4 are manual judgment for high-value leads only.** The governing stat: **"up to 2 follow-ups see results"** — follow-up #1 can raise reply probability, #3 lowers it; "Follow-ups are a probability game, not a moral virtue" (source: "LinkedIn Follow-Up Message: A Signal-Based System (+ Examples)"). (Note the "increase reply rates by 32%" figure in that source appears only inside a hypothetical sample message — not a benchmark.)

### The follow-up priority rule
Decide effort by lead value (source: "LinkedIn Follow-Up Message: A Signal-Based System (+ Examples)"):
- **High-value** (strong ICP, buying authority, strategic logo, engagement signals) → manual contextual follow-up.
- **Medium-value** → structured automation (cap 2–3 steps, 7+ day delays, auto-tag, remove after the limit).
- **Low-value / low-signal** → exit early, exclude, withdraw stale requests.

The discipline is to **enforce restraint through campaign design, not "smart AI"** — cap steps, set minimum delays, use aggressive exclude lists, and run a lightweight weekly review. "Build a system where over-messaging becomes structurally impossible."

### Weekly follow-up workflow (~20–30 min)
(source: "LinkedIn Follow-Up Message: A Signal-Based System (+ Examples)") (1) Filter the inbox for threads where you sent last and got no reply 7+ days ago; (2) cut to the 10–15 highest-value; (3) quick signal check (active? posted? role change?); (4) decide/tag/move on. **Track one metric: reply rate on manual follow-ups; re-evaluate after 4 weeks.**

## Relationship-first follow-up (Vuk's framework)

For the "after connecting" moment, HeyReach's CMO Vuk teaches a **relationship-first** sequence (self-reported; internal COI) (source: "How to Write a Follow-Up LinkedIn Message & Email [Tactics + Examples]"): keep the connection request **empty** (performs best) → use Sales Navigator to spot who viewed/followed/requested → start a **0%-sales human message** → ask a qualifying question → sell only if a legit opportunity, otherwise just converse. Sample: "Hi [Name]! Thanks for connecting. Btw, I'm a massive fan of [tool]... paying respects where respect is due." Once someone replies, "I take over and reply myself. No AI-generated replies for my leads."

## Follow-up cadence and timing

The recurring "money is in the follow-ups" theme, with cadences that space out (LinkedIn keeps permanent history, so don't spam):

- **Up to ~3 touchpoints** to get a response; each must **add new value**, not rehash; a simple **👋 emoji** works as a bump (source: "How to Write a Follow-Up LinkedIn Message & Email [Tactics + Examples]").
- **Timing:** email ~10 AM or 2 PM; follow up 2–3 days after the initial, then again after 3–5 days; after-meeting follow-ups within 24 hours; after-event within 1–3 days (source: "How to Write a Follow-Up LinkedIn Message & Email [Tactics + Examples]").
- A longer-gap cadence from a practitioner: day 1 first message → +2 days ("is this a priority?") → +4 days (just "[name]?" or a micro case study) → +30 days ("pinging this back up") → then 60/90 days — long gaps "so you don't burn the infinite DM thread" (source: "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)").
- **3-step inbound cadence:** initial DM → follow-up in 2 days → final nudge 7 days later (source: "LinkedIn lead generation strategy for 60%+ reply rates").
- **Break up after 2–3 unanswered follow-ups** — "a good salesperson knows when to withdraw and break up" (source: "How to set up LinkedIn drip campaigns that actually generate leads").

## The graceful-exit / breakup message

Every follow-up sequence should end with a deliberate breakup that **says it's the last message, gives an easy out, and leaves the door open** — it "generates more replies than expected" and surfaces soft objections (a "reach out in a month" is a near-certain future booking) (source: "Sales sequence automation: Fewer steps, more replies"; source: "These 4 LinkedIn Messages Booked Me 100+ Calls (Copy These)"). See the "pitch and exit" beat in [[linkedin-message-formulas]].

## Summary

Follow up by **state, not calendar**: don't bump a pending request or an unreplied opener more than once, wait for a *new signal* before re-touching an active-but-silent lead, and reserve manual, contextual follow-ups for the warmest and highest-value threads. Cap automated sequences at 2–3 steps with 7+ day delays, make every touch add something new, end with a graceful breakup, and take over manually the moment someone replies. Over-following is the mistake the frameworks are built to prevent.
