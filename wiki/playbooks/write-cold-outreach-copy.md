# Writing Cold Outreach Copy That Gets Replies

How to write LinkedIn connection requests, first messages, and follow-ups people actually answer. The through-line across every source: **who you message and whether the message is relevant matters far more than clever phrasing** — but once targeting is right, a few disciplined copy moves (open with an observation, ask an easy question, remove reasons to say no, write like a human) reliably lift reply rates. This is the practice of writing copy; the *named* formulas behind it are collected in [[linkedin-message-formulas]], and the campaign mechanics that carry the copy are in [[build-linkedin-campaign-sequences]].

## First principle: relevance beats personalization beats phrasing

Practitioners repeatedly say tweaking words barely moves reply rate — "who you reach out to matters way more than what you say" (source: "I Made $203,874.03 From LinkedIn Outreach and Learned This…"). A HeyReach team member reframes personalization as **relevance**: the reason you picked *this* person, the pain they have now, so "this message could not have been sent to anybody else," and warns that old-style personalization (their university, hobbies) "can easily cross the boundary of personal and professional life" (source: "The Best LinkedIn Outreach Strategy for 2026"). Kellen Kaspar of The Deal Lab (agency, COI) puts the sharpest version: "**tokenization is not personalization**" — inserting `{{first name}}` isn't experienced as personal, whereas a cluster-level truth ("you focus on eating by macronutrient profile") resonates (source: "These 4 LinkedIn Messages Booked Me 100+ Calls (Copy These)").

## The connection request

**Leave it blank in most cases.** Empty connection requests are the most repeated single tactic in the corpus. HeyReach's own data claims empty requests beat personalized notes by ~5% (from its 96,051-campaign report) and, in another cut, 27% acceptance blank vs 22% with text (vendor data, attribute as such) (source: "How to CRUSH the New LinkedIn Algorithm in 10 Minutes"; source: "How to turn post reactors into a high-converting lead list in 30 minutes"). A note "triggers the sales part of the brain" and raises the guard (source: "This LinkedIn System Booked Me 80 Qualified Sales Calls in 30 Days"). Two practical reasons reinforce it: on free LinkedIn you can only personalize a handful of requests before they send empty anyway, and note character limits are tight — **200 chars on free, 300 on Premium / Sales Navigator / Recruiter** (source: "11 LinkedIn connection message templates that people actually accept").

**When to add a note:** when your offer is genuinely irresistible (a strong guarantee/free trial), pitch it outright and ask for the call (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages"); or for a warm/urgent, non-saturated niche where timing is the hook. The "11 templates" post lays out four note strategies — empty, common ground, a *specific* genuine compliment (generic praise "reads as automated and does more harm than good"), and short-and-sweet — and calls mentioning a mutual connection "one of the best-performing" (source: "11 LinkedIn connection message templates that people actually accept").

## The first message after acceptance

Goal: **get a reply, not book a meeting** (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages"). The most-cited structure is **Observation → Question**: a short, specific reason you reached out, then an easy-to-answer question, kept entirely about them and short/scannable (source: "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)"; source: "How to Get Clients on LinkedIn in 2026 (30+ Clients Per Month)"). HeyReach's "5 million DMs" playbook expands the manual version to **Compliment (personalized) + Observation (relevant reason) + easy Question** (source: "I Sent 5,000,000 LinkedIn DMs: here's what you need to know").

Repeated tactical moves:

- **Give before you ask.** The first message should hand over value (a tip, resource, or the done-for-you lead magnet) so you're "not the 100th person asking for a call" (source: "I Made $203,874.03 From LinkedIn Outreach and Learned This…"); Josh Braun's "deposit before withdrawal" is the named version credited by Kellen Kaspar (source: "These 4 LinkedIn Messages Booked Me 100+ Calls (Copy These)").
- **Shift from "I" to "you."** Mind the "you-to-I ratio" (credited to Josh Braun) — write "you look like you're doing X" not "I/we/my product" (source: "Copy This LinkedIn Message Formula, It’ll Blow Up your Inbox"; source: "The ONLY LinkedIn Lead Generation Video You Need").
- **Ask a "this vs that" or "poke-the-bear" question.** A two-option question ("scaling right now or trying to be more profitable?") is low-friction (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages"); a "poke the bear" question makes the prospect feel a pain rather than stating a feature — e.g. "how do you know you're not being overcharged... on your current transactions with Stripe?" (Tim Jacobson, B2B Boosted, COI) (source: "Clay + HeyReach Integration Tutorial: Personalized LinkedIn Outreach at Scale (Step-by-Step)").
- **Qualify quietly with proxy metrics.** Ask "how many new ad creatives do you launch monthly?" instead of prying about ad spend (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages").
- **Use social proof as a subtle micro-line**, not a case-study link — "had a client in this niche, got them to 2x ROAS, shall I show you?" (source: "LinkedIn DM Strategy: What Works After 5,000,000 Messages").
- **Delay links, decks and calendar invites.** Early DMs are for understanding pain, not sending resources or uninvited calendar links (source: "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)").

## Named message architectures (attribute these)

- **Full story → agitation → pitch-and-exit** (Kellen Kaspar / The Deal Lab, COI): after acceptance a "full story" message (saw this → thought this → here's what I'm offering); if silent, a fair-but-direct "agitation" question ("Is outbound not a meaningful part of your pipeline generation strategy right now?"); then a softened breakup that gives permission to decline (source: "Copy This LinkedIn Message Formula, It’ll Blow Up your Inbox").
- **The four message *types*** (Kellen Kaspar / The Deal Lab, COI): Occam's Razor (as simple as possible, passes the "spouse barbecue test"), Offer-led (lead with value, e.g. give the product free), Agitational bump (a context-free follow-up that makes them scroll up — plays off Justin Michael's "thought bump"), and softened Breakup pitch. Underpinned by "Message Market Fit" — "what they will say yes to" — built on Brian Balfour's four-quadrants-of-market-fit essays (source: "These 4 LinkedIn Messages Booked Me 100+ Calls (Copy These)").
- **The five-message framework** (Tim / B2B Boosted, COI): (1) greeting, (2) a controlled compliment "with five out of 10 level of enthusiasm," (3) an authority observation, (4) a curiosity question, (5) a resonance/transition line — send message one immediately, then wait 30–60 min (source: "Claude + LinkedIn = Unlimited Leads on Autopilot"; source: "How I Personalize 10,000+ LinkedIn Messages With AI in Seconds").
- **Remove landmines / via negativa** (Nassim Taleb, credited by Kellen Kaspar; "simple & clear" credited to Will Allred of Lavender): steal the objection and put it in your copy so there's nothing left to say no to (source: "Copy This LinkedIn Message Formula, It’ll Blow Up your Inbox"). ## Six proven cold-DM scripts (each attributed to its author)

From an analysis of ~1,000,000 cold DMs, six scripts with their authors and self-reported results (unverified; each author has a tool COI) (source: "Cold DMs That Actually Work in 2026 (1M Messages Analyzed)"):

1. **Long, social-proof-heavy** (presenter's favorite): "hey name + why you're reaching out + social proof + offer" — say your proof "really loudly and with your chest."
2. **Dogfooding** (Sammy Barbier ↔ GenPage): use your own product to sell it; frontloading value got ~2× meetings despite fewer raw replies (22 positive replies *with* the personalized page vs 47 *without*, but more booked meetings).
3. **Job-signal + multi-threading** (Adash ↔ Bitscale): message off a funded, explicit pain (hiring a GTM engineer) and reach multiple seniority levels; 311 leads → 26 meetings.
4. **Peer/event invite** (presenter): invite same-title peers to a dinner; non-salesy.
5. **Free-leads marketplace** (Joe Wy): give the prospect both a contact list *and* ready-to-send messages; 254 leads → 30% acceptance, 25% reply, 15 calls in 7 days.
6. **Direct lead magnet** (Tim Jacobson ↔ B2B Boosted): "I have this crazy lead magnet that [does X]. Can I send it over? Simply reply yes" — the magnet must be "stupidly valuable" and target a problem/solution-aware person.

Persona-specific note copy (different message per GTM role, because "Connection ≠ Context") is templated in HeyReach's ICP Outreach Vault — the point is role-fit over phrasing, e.g. a founder-led CFO campaign self-reported a 50% reply rate on 134 leads (source: "Persona-perfect LinkedIn outreach templates: Steal our ICP flows").

## Write like a human (anti-AI tells)

Casual beats formal — write "like you typed it on WhatsApp," use abbreviations and light punctuation to pattern-interrupt LinkedIn's formality (source: "I Sent 5,000,000 LinkedIn DMs: here's what you need to know"; source: "How I made $5 Million with LinkedIn Outreach (1 Hour Masterclass)"). Practitioners also advise the "more unplanned it looks, the better... it screams less automation" (source: "The Best LinkedIn Outreach Strategy for Agencies in 2026"). Specific AI-tells to strip: never write "synergy," "revolutionize" or AI buzzwords, and remove em dashes (source: "How I Personalize 10,000+ LinkedIn Messages With AI in Seconds"; source: "I Built an AI Agent in n8n That Books Sales Calls For You"). On whether to use AI at all, Kellen Kaspar's caution: "bad use is worse than no use," because "people can sniff AI copy from a mile off" — see [[ai-personalization-at-scale]].

## Follow-up copy

Never reword the first message — add a new reason to reply (source: "The Laziest Way to Book Meetings on LinkedIn"). Two moves recur: a single waving-emoji "👋" bump that "feels superhuman" and drove many replies in HeyReach's own 42%-reply campaign, then a short "would love to get an answer to the question above 👆" (source: "LinkedIn message automation: the playbook that got us a 42% reply rate"); and a humorous **breakup** message as the last touch, which "works better than it has any right to" (source: "LinkedIn outreach strategy for 2026: How to build campaigns that don’t die after week two").
