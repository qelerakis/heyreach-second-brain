- **strategies/playbooks:** Build a "LinkedIn-first" outbound engine (LinkedIn primary, email as support/fallback). **Four layers:** (1) Targeting & enrichment — "if your data isn't rich enough to write a personalized first line, your targeting isn't ready"; (2) Warming — view profiles, engage/comment before the connection request; (3) Outreach & follow-up — structured sequences with a shared team framework; (4) Conversion — book meetings, sync to CRM, extend non-converters to email. **Foundation before scaling:** sharpen a LinkedIn-specific ICP (role/seniority/company stage/industry + at least one trigger signal), validate in Sales Navigator (ask "is this audience large enough to sustain weekly campaigns for 3+ months?"), optimize rep profiles (headline as value prop e.g. "Helping B2B sales teams fix their outbound pipeline", ~1–2 hrs/rep), build peer-to-peer messaging architecture, set non-negotiable daily activity thresholds. **Five-stage execution framework:** Stage 1 Signal-based targeting (Sales Nav core filters + Clay enrichment for tech stack/funding/hiring + RB2B for website visitors + Trigify for job changes/content/engagement; require ≥2 personalization hooks per prospect before entering a campaign; import to HeyReach via CSV/Sales Nav URL/native integration; sender rotation). Stage 2 Strategic warming (engage 2–3 times over a week before the request; HeyReach controls timing/spacing so warming isn't in "suspicious bursts"). Stage 3 Connection & opening conversation (send personalized message within 24–48h of acceptance; "it's not a pitch"; HeyReach auto-triggers next step on acceptance). Stage 4 Value-driven follow-up (conditional logic: Connected but didn't reply → Follow-up A; Viewed profile but silent → Follow-up B; Replied → exit to live conversation; follow-ups fire at 3–5 day intervals). Stage 5 Meeting conversion & multichannel extension (Unibox to manage all replies; route non-converters into parallel email sequences via Zapier or Make carrying LinkedIn context). **Diagnostic decision tree:** low acceptance + low reply = targeting problem; high acceptance + low reply = messaging problem; high acceptance + good reply + few meetings = conversion problem; everything decent but volume too low = infrastructure problem (add accounts/activity within safe limits). Run this diagnostic weekly.

## Low reply rate on LinkedIn? Use RTA to find exactly what's broken
- **source_type:** blog_post
- **source_url:** https://www.heyreach.io/blog/low-reply-rate-on-linkedin
- **topics:** linkedin-outreach, analytics-benchmarks, copywriting-messaging, sequences-campaigns, inbox-reply-management, heyreach-product, targeting-icp-lists
- **strategies/playbooks:** Introduces **RTA = Reply-to-Acceptance rate = (Replies ÷ Accepted connections) × 100** to separate targeting from messaging (reply rate is a "blended number"). Calculate per campaign, not blended (HeyReach Dashboard → select campaign → Export CSV; Google Sheets =B2/A2*100 where A=accepted, B=replies). Read only with enough volume: patterns stabilize ~100 accepted connections/campaign; A/B tests need ~50 accepted per variation. **3 RTA patterns:** Pattern 1 — Low & flat RTA (below ~15%) across all campaigns = opening message is failing after connection → rewrite Step 1 to anchor on one specific problem (not value prop/meeting ask), make CTA near-zero-commitment (a question, not a calendar link); A/B via Campaign Builder → Step 1 → Add Message Variation. Pattern 2 — RTA drops across campaign duration = follow-up sequence too weak → check sequence depth (fewer than 3 steps after acceptance is the likely gap; follow-up must add a new angle, not "just circling back"); HeyReach lacks native time-series RTA so export & compare reply volume week over week; use Unibox to find non-replied conversations and route into a fresh sequence with a new Step 1. Pattern 3 — RTA varies significantly across campaigns = ICP–message fit problem → sort campaigns by RTA, compare audience filters (role/company size/seniority) between top and bottom performers, rewrite the opener's problem per ICP. **Priority order:** (1) if acceptance is low, fix targeting first (get acceptance above ~25% before using RTA); (2) Pattern 1 (highest-probability); (3) Pattern 3; (4) Pattern 2 last. Broken vs fixed opener example provided.
- **frameworks/methods/tools:** RTA formula & pattern model; CSV export + Google Sheets; A/B via Add Message Variation.
- **product/features:** HeyReach Dashboard/Export CSV; **Positive Reply Detection / Positive-reply detection** (adds an intent layer on top of RTA, auto-separating positive replies from generic responses or polite objections); Campaign Builder → Step 1 → Add Message Variation; Unibox (identify non-replied conversations in real time, build re-engage workflows). Note: RTA is "not a native dashboard metric."
- **opinions/hot-takes:** "Reply rate tells you what happened. RTA tells you where it broke." "A first message that names a real problem builds trust — a pitch tears it down before the conversation starts." Response rate as a diagnostic (vs outcome) is a mistake.
- **numbers/benchmarks:** 96,051 campaigns in HeyReach dataset; 10.7% of campaigns with accepted connections got **zero** replies. RTA benchmarks: ~18% typical, below ~10% weak ("something is broken"), above ~28% strong ICP-message fit. Acceptance target ~25% (elsewhere ~25–30%). A/B: ≥50 accepted per variation; ~100 accepted for directional signal. Example table: Campaign A = Heads of Sales (50–200 employees) 25% RTA (fits ICP); Campaign B = RevOps (enterprise) 4% (doesn't resonate). Pattern 1 threshold ~<15%.
- **people/companies:** HeyReach. (Roles referenced: Head of Sales vs RevOps lead — different ICPs.)
- **voice/quotes:** "Not low replies — zero." "Unfocused outreach efforts are noise until you know which layer is broken." Broken opener ❌ "would love to connect and explore how we might work together"; fixed ✅ names a specific problem ("curious how you're handling reply drop-offs after connection acceptance").
