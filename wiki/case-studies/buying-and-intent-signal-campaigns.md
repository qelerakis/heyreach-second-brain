# Buying- and intent-signal campaigns (funding, hiring, tool & real-time triggers)

Where [[engagement-signal-campaigns]] target people who engaged with *content*, these campaigns target people (or accounts) where **something just changed** — a company is hiring, a round closed, a tool was added/dropped, a new leader started, or a prospect is tracking a market conversation. HeyReach's blog frames the shift as moving "from 'who fits my ICP' to 'who fits my ICP and something just changed'" (source: "Outbound ICP signals: How to spot high-intent leads before your competitors"). Every study below is on HeyReach's own playbook library, and in almost every case the practitioner is running **their own signal tool** — a conflict of interest flagged per study. All numbers are self-reported and unverified.

## Francesco Corti (Karhuno AI) — 58 positive replies with two tools

**Who:** **Francesco Corti**, a Business Development Manager and founder/user of **Karhuno AI** (karhuno.com); HeyReach crowned him an "Outbound Outlier." He "eats his own dog food," using his own product to run the campaign — a clear COI (source: "58 positive replies using real-time buying signals").

**Context & tactic:** Only **two tools** — Karhuno + HeyReach. Karhuno's "LinkedIn hot leads finder" surfaces content the audience engages with and exports post engagers matching criteria (e.g. C-level only); it also scans public content (press releases, articles, job boards) with plain-English filters to find real-time signals (hiring, funding, tool mentions). HeyReach runs a deliberately non-salesy **3-message** sequence: connection request with a personalized note referencing their comment → DM introducing the tool with a free invite → one follow-up with the link, "100% free during beta" (source: "58 positive replies using real-time buying signals").

**Numbers (self-reported):** **151 leads total; 95 connections accepted (~63% acceptance); 58 positive replies.** The page's "38%+ positive reply rate" is 58/151 — a positive-reply share of *total leads*, not a reply rate on messages sent; note the denominator (source: "58 positive replies using real-time buying signals").

**Their conclusion / limits:** Qualify hard — "why waste time on people who can't sign checks?" Just 3 messages; results are from his own tool used on himself.

## Bitscale — 26 meetings from job-posting signals

**Who:** **Bitscale** (bitscale.ai) founders/GTM operators **Sanket Goyal, Abhinay Kumar, and Yash Sharma**, running their **own** tool connected to HeyReach — a COI (source: "Founder led sales: turn signals into conversations").

**Context & tactic:** A small founder-led team turning **job postings** into outreach — a hiring post is "a company holding up a sign that says 'Please sell me something to fix this specific issue.'" Bitscale finds companies posting relevant roles and its AI Agent writes contextual messages; leads + messages flow to HeyReach. They ran **multi-threaded ABM**, targeting executives, decision-makers, and champions per account with different AI-written messages. HeyReach's "If Connection" split routed already-connected leads to instant messaging (+ two 48-hour follow-ups) and strangers through a connection request first (source: "Founder led sales: turn signals into conversations").

**Numbers (self-reported):** **Connections sent 311; accepted 143 (46% acceptance); messages sent 133; replies 49 (37% reply rate); 26 meetings booked.** Reaching out on the job signal "doubled their acceptance rates" (their claim) (source: "Founder led sales: turn signals into conversations").

**Their conclusion / limits:** Timing beats volume; small team scaled reach via multi-threading. Depends on live job-posting signals (era-bound).

## LevelUp Leads — an "Evergreen Hiring Campaign" (companies that are hiring)

**Who:** **Matthew Coleman**, GTM Engineer at **LevelUp Leads** (levelupleads.io) — his firm is the COI (source: "Hiring campaign that generates 100s of replies on autopilot").

**Context & tactic:** Note the terminology: this targets **companies that are hiring** (a buying signal), not candidate recruiting. A Clay "signal" continuously finds new job postings each month and auto-feeds a table → HeyReach (LinkedIn) + Smartlead (email). For LevelUp Leads, companies hiring SDRs/BDRs = companies investing in outbound. Heavy data-cleaning for deliverability (title standardization, name trimming), Clay's "Find Contacts at Company" to pull 5–6 decision-makers per company, and **Octave** to personalize from both the job post and the prospect's profile (source: "Hiring campaign that generates 100s of replies on autopilot").

**Numbers (self-reported, qualitative):** **5–6 contacts per hiring company; connects with "thousands of prospects" monthly; generates "hundreds of replies" automatically.** No precise reply/acceptance rate is given — the results are round/qualitative volumes, not measured rates (source: "Hiring campaign that generates 100s of replies on autopilot").

**Their conclusion / limits:** "There's no better signal than when a company actively hires for roles that indicate they need your solution." Set-once, run-forever. Results are unquantified.

## Ashvin Jankee (Signado) — 47.8% acceptance on 23 signal-triggered leads (with a control)

**Who:** **Ashvin Jankee**, founder of **Signado** (signado.io), dogfooding his own signal-detection tool — COI. The page carries a strong first-person HeyReach endorsement (proxy infrastructure, "the obvious choice for our send layer") = a vendor-favorable testimonial (source: "How Ashvin used buying signals to turn LinkedIn contacts into meetings").

**Context & tactic:** Built on the **HeyReach API**. Thesis: "most personalization is cosmetic" — real personalization references an actual event. Signado monitors **7 signal types** (funding, hiring surges, leadership changes, LinkedIn posts, product launches, partnerships, press) and backfills 6–12 months so contacts with existing signals queue immediately. Message = first name + a specific icebreaker naming the real signal + a soft question; no generic praise. A key lesson: **sequence timing is part of personalization** — a DM 3 hours after acceptance "signals automation," so batch 2 stretched the post-acceptance delay from 3 hours to 3 days (source: "How Ashvin used buying signals to turn LinkedIn contacts into meetings").

**Numbers (self-reported):** **Batch 1 (23 signal-triggered leads): 47.8% connection acceptance, 36.4% reply rate among accepted, 2 meetings booked.** The control — parallel generic connect-only campaigns on the **same account** — got **17–21% acceptance and 0% replies.** Batch 2 (353 contacts) results were explicitly **PENDING** and must not be recorded as a result (source: "How Ashvin used buying signals to turn LinkedIn contacts into meetings").

**Their conclusion / limits:** The signal-vs-control contrast (0% replies on generic outreach from the same account) is the headline evidence, but batch 1 is a **tiny sample (23 leads → 2 meetings)**; the scaled test is unfinished.

## "Ben" (traxy) — from 25% to 58% acceptance via intent signals

**Who:** A single practitioner, **"Ben"** (thin attribution, one LinkedIn account), featuring the **traxy ↔ HeyReach** integration — traxy is the co-promoted partner/COI (source: "Go from 25% to 58% acceptance rates using LinkedIn intent signals").

**Context & tactic:** Ben ran HeyReach fine but was stuck at 20–25% acceptance because Sales Navigator lists matched a profile without behavior. Fix: target on behavior. **traxy** watches people engaging with tracked content (his posts, competitors', influencers', market discussions), qualifies them against the ICP, and pushes them into a chosen HeyReach campaign **while intent is fresh** (paste HeyReach API key, pick a campaign, set auto delivery). To prove lift, the first three weeks used Sales Nav targeting only, then traxy was added mid-campaign. He ran a **single sender capped at 27 connection requests/day** (source: "Go from 25% to 58% acceptance rates using LinkedIn intent signals").

**Numbers (self-reported) — read the windows carefully:**

| Window | Acceptance | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline (Sales Nav only, first 3 weeks) | 20–25% | — |
| All-time average (incl. slow early weeks) | 45% | — |
| Last 30 days | 56% | 27% |
| Last 7 days | 58% | 28% |

Also: **19 qualified meetings from ONE profile over 2 months** (source: "Go from 25% to 58% acceptance rates using LinkedIn intent signals").

**Metric caveat to preserve:** the "25% → 58%" headline compares the Sales-Nav baseline against the **best 7-day window** — the all-time average is 45%. Cite the window. The 27-requests/day cap is framed as a safe 2025-era LinkedIn limit (source: "Go from 25% to 58% acceptance rates using LinkedIn intent signals").
